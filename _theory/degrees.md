# Key Scale Degrees (*Darajeh-hā-ye Mohem*)

A Persian *maqām* is defined not merely by the pitches of its *dāng* or actual scale, but by the **functional hierarchy** (*selseleh-marāteb-e darajeh-hā*) governing how those pitches behave in melody. Five named scale degrees—each represented by a standard Persian shorthand letter in transcriptions and analytical tables—establish the gravitational center, phrasing, inflection, and closure of every *gūsheh*.

---

## 1. Overview of the Five Key Degrees

| Degree | Persian | Symbol | Core Function | Modal Importance |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Shāhed** (Witness) | شاهد | **ش** | Primary focal pitch; center of melodic gravity and emphasis. | **Highest** — defines the *maqām*. |
| **Khātemeh** (Finalis) | خاتمه | **خ** | Definitive final resting pitch signaling complete closure in the mother *maqām*. | **High** — anchors final resolution. |
| **Īst** (Pause) | ایست | **ی** | Temporary resting pitch where a phrase pauses while anticipating continuation. | **Context-dependent** — shapes phrase tension. |
| **Moteghayyer** (Variable) | متغیر | **م** | Pitch altered upward or downward during performance due to melodic attraction. | **Context-dependent** — adds directional color. |
| **Āghāz** (Initial) | آغاز | **آ** | Opening pitch that launches the melodic trajectory. | **Lowest** — least determinative of mode. |

## 2. *Shāhed* (شاهد — Symbol: **ش**)

The ***shāhed*** ("witness" or "testifier") is the degree emphasized more than any other pitch in a *maqām*. It serves as the central axis around which the melody revolves and toward which melodic motion consistently gravitates.

* **Single vs. Dual *Shāhed*:** Most *maqāms* have a single *shāhed*—for example, *Darāmad II of Navā* (from the *radif* of Mirzā Abdollāh). However, certain *maqāms* operate with **two *shāheds***, such as *Darāmad I of Rāst-Panjgāh* (Mirzā Abdollāh).
* **Decisive Modal Role:** Among all five degrees, the *shāhed* carries the **greatest weight** in identifying a *maqām* and shaping its modal ethos (*eḥsās-e modāl*). Its literal meaning—*that which bears witness*—underscores this role: it testifies to which *maqām* is being performed. Changing the *shāhed* changes the *maqām* itself.

## 3. *Īst* (ایست — **ی**) and *Khātemeh* (خاتمه — **خ**)

Both *īst* and *khātemeh* are resting degrees where the melody comes to a stop, distinguished by whether the closure is temporary or final:

### *Khātemeh* (Finalis — **خ**)
* When a melodic stop is **definitive and final**, giving the listener a clear sense that the piece or *gūsheh* has concluded, that pitch is the ***khātemeh***.
* Because every *maqām* in classical Persian music belongs to a *dastgāh* or *āvāz*, a true sense of ultimate finality arises only when the music returns to the **mother *maqām*** (*darāmad*). Consequently, the *khātemeh* belongs properly to the mother *maqām* of the *dastgāh* or *āvāz*.
* **Multiple Finales:** Certain *dastgāhs* or *āvāzes* can conclude on more than one *khātemeh*—most notably ***Āvāz-e Afshārī***.

### *Īst* (Pause — **ی**)
* When a melodic stop is **temporary**, leaving the listener expecting the piece to continue, that pitch is an ***īst***.
* A single *maqām* may possess **more than one *īst*** degree (as seen alongside the *khātemeh* in *Darāmad II of Navā* from Mirzā Abdollāh's *radif*), allowing performers to build suspended cadences and multi-phrase arcs before resolving to the *khātemeh*.

---

## 4. *Moteghayyer* (متغیر — Symbol: **م**)

The ***moteghayyer*** ("variable" or "changing") is a scale degree whose pitch height shifts during the course of a piece.

* **Melodic Attraction:** This alteration is governed by the gravitational pull of neighboring degrees. In ascending versus descending melodic lines, the performer raises or lowers the *moteghayyer* so that it leans closer to the next target pitch.
* **Classic Example:** *Darāmad of Dashtī* (from Mirzā Abdollāh's *radif*) prominently illustrates the *moteghayyer*, where a degree inflects upward in ascent and relaxes downward in descent.

## 5. *Āghāz* (آغاز — Symbol: **آ**)

The ***āghāz*** ("beginning" or "initial") is the pitch on which a piece or *gūsheh* begins, setting the initial direction of the melody (e.g., *Darāmad of Chahārgāh* in Mirzā Abdollāh's *radif*).

* **Flexibility:** A single *gūsheh*, *dastgāh*, or *āvāz* may begin on different *āghāz* degrees across performances or transmissions.
* **Relative Weight:** Among the five named degrees, the *āghāz* has the **least impact** on determining modal identity, whereas the *shāhed* has the greatest, and *khātemeh*, *īst*, and *moteghayyer* assume varying degrees of importance depending on the specific *maqām*.
